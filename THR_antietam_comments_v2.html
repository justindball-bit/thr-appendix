I am a clinical psychologist. I specialize in combat trauma. I have worked with veterans for twenty three years.

The young soldier at the end. After the shell. After he finds the older man. The description everyone keeps using is that his eyes are present but he isn't there. I want to give that a name.

What you are seeing is acute dissociative response to traumatic loss under ongoing threat. It is one of the most well documented psychological responses in combat literature. The body stays functional — he gets to his feet, he responds to the officer — but the self temporarily evacuates the premises. It is not shock in the medical sense. It is the mind doing exactly what it is supposed to do when reality becomes briefly unprocessable. Protecting itself by creating distance between the person and what just happened.

What is remarkable about this footage is not that it shows this response. It is that it shows it correctly. The specific quality of that disconnection — present but absent, functional but hollow — is almost impossible to perform convincingly. It is not something you act. It is something that happens to you.

I don't know what this footage is. I know that young man's face is real.
